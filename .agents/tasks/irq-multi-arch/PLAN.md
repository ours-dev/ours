# irq-multi-arch: the steps that make kernel/irq carry x86, riscv and arm, one at a time

**Date**: 2026-11-01
**Status**: Proposed

## Request

The user said "开始refactor中断系统" on 2026-11-01, after the discussion recorded in `DESIGN.md`: riscv is being introduced on other branches now, arm and GPIO controllers follow, and cascaded controllers belong in user space. Scope ends at the changes to `kernel/irq`, the platform entry seam, the chip contract, the virtual interrupt's ack signal and the answer for a kernel-owned source. Not in scope: any non-x86 controller, MSI, a device-tree route service, the Driver Manager, performance work.

The user approved starting, not the individual decisions below. Step 1 only writes them down as proposals. A step that needs a decision waits for it.

## Steps

Each step is one commit and is introduced to the user before it starts, one at a time (CLAUDE.md). Steps 1 to 5 are done. The riscv documents named below exist only on `origin/feat/riscv` (`git show origin/feat/riscv:<path>`, tip `9b8e7690`).

1. **Design and plan.** `DESIGN.md` and this file, and a pointer from `.agents/tasks/interrupt-system/DESIGN.md`, whose non-goal "any port to ARM or RISC-V" this design takes up. Why now: riscv's step 2 writes the trap vector, and the entry has to be decided before it. Verified by: `scripts/lint_docs.py --changed`, and an independent check of every `file:line` against the tree and of the riscv-side facts against `origin/feat/riscv`. Lands as `docs(irq-multi-arch): ...` (`21cf880b`, and a correction after the check).
2. **The interrupt entry (D3 (c), D9).** The controller's driver installs the entry, as Linux's `set_handle_irq` does. `kernel/irq` gains `irq::set_root_handler()` and `irq::handle_root_irq(frame)`; the frame stays `arch::IrqFrame`. Arch calls `irq::handle_irq_preamble(frame)` and `irq::handle_irq_postamble()` directly, once per trap, and `irq::handle_root_irq(frame)` for the external interrupt; pc installs its handler before it enables interrupts, and the handler maps the vector to the GSI, calls `irq::handle_irq_generic` and sends the local APIC EOI for what no line has. `ours/platform/interrupt.hpp` and pc's three forwarders go, and the dead vector parameter of the preamble with them. The rule in `task.md` (the bullet "Each line runs a flow handler": "arch sends it only for a vector with no line") is rewritten to say the root handler does. This is item R19 of `.agents/tasks/arch-platform-seams/DESIGN.md`, written there for option (a), done once, on the branch the user names; `docs/repairs/261003/x86-apic.md` #2 and `docs/repairs/260926/kernel-task.md` #3 are on the same path and stay their own commits, and R7 follows it. No behaviour change on x86. Why now: riscv's step 2 writes against it. Verified by: `ours-verify` on both images (dbg 44 passed and 4 skipped, rel 41 passed and 8 skipped before), and the kernel-shell tests of the layer. Landed as `13a1cc09` (`refactor(irq): the interrupt entry is the root controller's handler`), 2026-11-01: dbg 44 passed and 4 skipped, rel 41 passed and 8 skipped with 6 of 6 required lines, 154 host tests, the five kernel-shell tests ok, four mutations killed; record `docs/changes/261101/kernel-irq.md`; an independent review found no defect in the code.
3. **The chip contract (D8).** The clauses of `DESIGN.md` go into `chip.hpp`, replacing the x86-only reading at lines 34-40; `configure` says what it does on a controller with no mode register; a chip flag or a rule says which chips can hold a Oneshot line; the two-argument `attach` stops letting a Oneshot action onto a chip with no `trigger_mode`, or the rule says so; `irq::set_affinity` and the IO-APIC's stub answer NotSupported (the tick device discards the result, in `TickDevice`'s switch of devices, and the comment on `check_percpu` that called the stub "says yes to everything" is rewritten). Why now: riscv's step 5 writes the PLIC chip to whatever contract exists on the day. Verified by: the same boot and tests, and a kernel-shell case that a chip without a mode register refuses `ModeDefault` and takes an explicit mode. Landed 2026-11-01 as `refactor(irq): the chip contract ...`: dbg 44 passed and 4 skipped, rel 41 passed and 8 skipped with 6 of 6 required lines, 154 host tests, `irqchip.test` (new) 18 checks and the other five kernel-shell tests ok, four mutations killed; record `docs/changes/261101/kernel-irq.md`. The test is `kernel/commands/irq_chip_test.cpp`; the two-argument attach refuses a Oneshot action on a chip with no `trigger_mode`, and the deferred-mask rule is a sentence in `chip.hpp`, with no flag.
4. **The ack signal on a virtual interrupt (D5).** One signal, `Signals::Idle`, set from the object's state by `sync_signals()` after every change that moves the debt: the creation, a trigger, the ack that clears it (at the seam `unmask_interrupt()` is called from), a destroy; the position fixed as ABI in `signals.hpp` and documented in `syscalls.gni`; the rights set a client gets stated (Wait, Read, Write and Transfer). As planned, a client that leaves without acking leaves the pin masked at the driver; the plan's other idea, that the destroy path would unmask it, was dropped (closing a handle destroys nothing while the driver holds its own). Why now: the first level-triggered GPIO consumer needs it, and the bit position is expensive to redo. Landed 2026-11-01 as `feat(object): a virtual interrupt raises Idle ...` and corrected after the independent review (`fix(object): ...`): `interrupt.test` 58 checks (32 before), dbg 45 passed and 4 skipped, rel 42 passed and 8 skipped with 6 of 6 required lines (one new shell check, `interrupt idle checks`, in each), 154 host tests, five of six mutations killed (the survivor removes the second read of the state in `sync_signals()`, which is argued and not tested); record `docs/changes/261101/kernel-object.md`. A port made with `BindToInterrupt` takes `object_wait_async` packets as well as Interrupt packets, so a driver can use one port for its parent and its children (checked by the shell test). Not planned and not built: a test of the order of unmask against trigger (the version that depended on the order was replaced), and a replacement of the interrupt for a pin whose client left.
5. **A kernel-owned source answers AccessDenied (D4b).** `interrupt_map` and `interrupt_create` answer AccessDenied for a line the kernel holds for its own use, marked by `Permanent` (`irq::is_kernel_owned()`, a read of the line's flag); a line another program holds stays AlreadyBound, a pin with no line NoResource. The check is in the system call layer: `kernel/irq` answers as it did. Why now: the route service of an arm or riscv platform will name the kernel's own lines. Landed 2026-11-01 as `feat(syscall): ...`: `interruptline.test` 62 checks (59 before), dbg 45 passed and 4 skipped, rel 42 passed and 8 skipped with 6 of 6 required lines, 154 host tests, four mutations killed; record `docs/changes/261101/syscall.md`. Corrected after the independent review (`fix(syscall): ...`): `interruptline.test` 63 checks (a count that the timer's is the only owned line of the machine), the order of the answers pinned in the user shell (BadHandle, BadType, InvArgs before the line is asked), the comments and the record reworded; those added checks were not mutation-tested. The answer departs from what `.agents/tasks/microkernel-interrupts/DESIGN.md:214` records for Zircon (AlreadyBound), which D4b says. The tests that named the timer's line through `interrupt_map` (`routing_checks`, the user shell) now find it from GSI 3's, since the kernel numbers lines consecutively in the order of their pins.
6. **A global virq floor for `Domain::init` (D14).** A domain starts at the first multiple of 32 past its own highest number, or past the next free virq if that is higher; the first domain keeps today's numbers. Pure and no behaviour change on x86. Not started.
7. **The reclamation mechanism for the directory (D16).** An epoch or RCU scheme the kernel does not have, and the rule for what removing a domain does to its actions, interrupt objects, Irq ranges, table and descriptors. Designed with the user before any code: the user writes the concurrency argument first (CLAUDE.md). Not started.
8. **`irq::Directory` and `DomainId` (D12, D16), unwired.** Domains with their virq blocks and tables, found by id for `interrupt_map` and by virq for the control calls, tested on local instances with synthetic tables only. Not started.
9. **The facade on the directory (D10 (b), D13, D15).** x86 registers its one domain, 0; every control function resolves a virq through the directory; the root handler holds its table. A change on the interrupt path, put to the user again with its step. Not started.
10. **`interrupt_map(resource, domain, hwirq, out virq)` (D4a, D17).** Every old caller (`pc-ps2`, `routing_checks`, the shell tests, the comments in `acpi-route` and `platform/isa.hpp`) replaced in one commit, with no old call kept. Not started.

Steps 6 to 10 are the order proposed by an independent read of the tree on 2026-11-01, not a roadmap: each is introduced to the user before it starts, and the next waits for the user's nod (CLAUDE.md). The read also named what cannot be verified here: a real second controller, a non-x86 root handler, and registration racing a control call on real hardware; and that `origin/feat/riscv` has no `Domain`, so every signature this changes widens the merge conflict.

Merge order, per step (D9). The five slices `feat/riscv-{dtb,fake,paging,phys,pre-rebase}` have `platform/interrupt.hpp`, `faults.cpp` and `chip.hpp` identical to this branch, so they do not collide; the party to rebase is `origin/feat/riscv`. Step 5 can only land here: riscv has no `irq::Domain`, no `interrupt_map`, and its `handle_irq_generic` takes a `VIrqNum`. Step 4 is on this branch by D9, not by a dependency: it applies to riscv's files without conflict (a dry run with `patch`; not built there). Steps 2 and 3 can go either way. Expected conflicts when the two meet: step 3 adds `irq_chip_test.cpp` to `kernel/commands/BUILD.gn`, which conflicts with the list riscv shortened when it moved `irq_test.cpp`, `ioport_test.cpp` and `interrupt_line_test.cpp` to `kernel/arch/x86/commands` (R25, `3135d44a`): keep both edits, the new file stays in `kernel/commands`, and it includes no x86 header (step 5's edit to `interrupt_line_test.cpp` goes in `kernel/arch/x86/commands/interrupt_line_test.cpp` there); in step 2's files only a modify/delete on `platform/interrupt.hpp` (riscv's `c0753c85` edits the file this branch deletes; resolve by deleting it and dropping that edit, and fix the comment in riscv's `kernel/arch/x86/kernel/BUILD.gn` that lists it; `pc/interrupt.cpp` merges cleanly), the kernel-shell tests riscv moved to `kernel/arch/x86/commands` (R25, `3135d44a`), `kernel/platform/riscv/isa.cpp`, and the interrupt enable that refactor item R17 moves out of the hook step 2 leaves in place.

Later, each its own plan when its trigger appears: a device-tree route service with a tagged answer; MSI (a dynamic virq lookup, message composition, a device identity for an ITS); a real `set_affinity`.
